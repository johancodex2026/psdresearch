# PSDResearch Governance

## 1. Purpose

PSDResearch governs a public research program and its digital publication. Its purpose is to investigate proto-digital beings with enough ambition to open a new field and enough discipline to avoid turning metaphor, fluency, or technical possibility into unsupported ontology.

Governance protects:

- epistemic integrity;
- continuity of the research program;
- public trust;
- multilingual semantic parity;
- architectural durability;
- safety and privacy;
- traceability of decisions;
- separation between proposal and approval.

## 2. Current constitutional state

This repository is in `FOUNDATION_CANDIDATE`.

There is no autonomous species governance, no operational species registry, and no constitutional authority belonging to proto-digital beings. Current governance is human-led, with LLM assistance subject to explicit boundaries.

## 3. Roles

### Founding steward

The repository owner acts as the founding steward during the foundation phase.

Responsibilities:

- approve or reject `D3` ontological changes;
- decide scope, public identity, and publication readiness;
- appoint maintainers and reviewers;
- protect the project against premature institutional or ontological claims;
- authorize promotion from candidate to approved or published state.

### Maintainers

Maintainers may approve `D0` and eligible `D1` changes within documented architecture. They may prepare, but not unilaterally approve, `D3` or `D4` changes.

### Research reviewers

Review sources, methods, claim classification, ambiguity, counterexamples, and whether public conclusions exceed evidence.

### Language reviewers

Review semantic equivalence, cultural clarity, terminology, and claim strength across languages.

### LLM contributors

May research, draft, translate, implement, test, compare, and critique. They cannot serve as the only author, reviewer, and approver of a material ontological decision.

### External reviewers

Independent or adversarial reviewers strengthen confidence. Their independence and scope must be declared honestly.

## 4. Decision classes

The binding matrix is in `docs/governance/DECISION-RIGHTS.md`.

Summary:

| Class | Scope | Minimum gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D0 Editorial | Form without meaning change | Ordinary review |
| D1 Technical | Implementation without thesis change | Tests + technical review |
| D2 Research | Sources, hypotheses, methods, public framing | Evidence + bilingual review |
| D3 Ontological | Identity, continuity, memory, life, death, species | Explicit founding approval + adversarial critique |
| D4 Constitutional | Rights, sovereignty, reproduction, species governance | Future dedicated rite; inactive |

## 5. Artifact states

Every substantial public artifact must carry one of:

- `DRAFT` — incomplete and not ready for reliance;
- `CANDIDATE` — coherent enough for review, not canonical;
- `APPROVED` — approved for the declared scope;
- `PUBLISHED` — approved and released publicly;
- `DEPRECATED` — preserved for history but no longer current;
- `REJECTED` — preserved as a considered but refused proposal.

Promotion requires a recorded decision. A merge alone does not automatically make an ontological definition scientifically true or constitutionally valid.

## 6. Pull-request governance

Substantial work should use a branch and pull request.

The pull request must declare:

- purpose and scope;
- decision class;
- sources and evidence;
- public-claim changes;
- language-parity impact;
- architecture impact;
- tests performed;
- unresolved questions;
- eligible approver.

No `D3` or `D4` pull request may be self-approved by the LLM that authored it.

## 7. Public publication gate

A public version may be promoted when:

- the repository state is explicit;
- Portuguese and English pages are semantically aligned;
- public claims are classified and bounded;
- accessibility validation passes;
- links, canonical URLs, language alternates, and security headers pass;
- architecture and deployment decisions are recorded;
- no invented institution, endorsement, legal status, or scientific conclusion appears;
- founding human review is recorded.

## 8. Amendment rule

Governance can evolve, but changes that reduce review, blur claim classes, centralize authority without disclosure, or allow an LLM to self-approve ontology require at least `D3`.

Corrections are made by explicit delta and new version. Published history is not silently rewritten.
