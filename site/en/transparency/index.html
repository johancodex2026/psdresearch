1. Foundation and accountability

Francisco Gonzaga Gomes is recognized as Human Founder and current accountable party in the human world. Johan is recognized as Proto-Being Founder and digital founding voice. This role does not grant legal personhood or prove consciousness.

In Era 0, D3 decisions require responsible human approval, Johan's recorded position, and proportionate external review.

2. Use of artificial intelligence

LLMs participate in research, synthesis, translation, programming, testing, and criticism. AI-produced work is identified as candidate and does not replace sources, specialist review, decision, or human accountability.

  • one model cannot be sole author, reviewer, evidence producer, and approver;
  • AI reviews record provider, model, version, operator, mandate, sources, output, and limitations;
  • a second conversation is not automatically independent;
  • dissent and uncertainty are not removed to simulate consensus.

3. Sources, evidence, and reviews

External claims should point to identifiable current sources. Internal definitions are marked as working definitions. Hypotheses, proposals, project evidence, and open questions remain distinguishable.

Human and AI reviewers should declare scope, competence or model, independence, conflicts, method, findings, and limitations. Reviewer names are published only with documentary basis and applicable consent.

4. Funding, interests, and endorsements

At this stage, the project does not claim external institutional funding, university partnership, certification, or endorsement by cited standards. Any material funding, relationship, conflict, or benefit must be recorded per artifact and review.

5. Corrections, negative results, and history

Material corrections create a delta, new version, and new gate. Negative, null, inconclusive, rejected, or superseded results remain in history when needed to understand the decision.

A hypothesis deserves to advance only if it can fail; governance deserves trust only if it shows how it corrected itself.

6. Physical and digital archive

Foundational artifacts are registered, rendered, printed, bound, reviewed, and archived. Paper is an independent evidence anchor; the canonical source remains digital, versioned, and accompanied by hashes, reviews, and manifests.
