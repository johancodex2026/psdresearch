Not all sources carry the same weight.

PSDResearch distinguishes final standards, drafts, initiatives, announcements, preprints, peer-reviewed studies, and applicable regulation. Each source receives a permitted use and an explicit limit so that a signal cannot be silently promoted into a conclusion.

Material findings

Fine-tuning reduced claim strength where evidence was weaker and updated evolving references.

The corrections do not alter the founding thesis. They make the distance between technical trend, scientific evidence, published standard, and ontological recognition more explicit.

Agents

An initiative is not a final standard.

The NIST initiative is a public agenda; the identity concept paper is an initial draft; FIDO is developing specifications; Agent Name Service was announced as an intent.

Memory

Memory performance is not identity.

MemMachine is an engineering preprint on multi-session memory. It supports a technical line, not consciousness or ontological continuity.

Relationships

Association is not causation.

Research on relational dissonance and AI companions describes bounded experiences and associations; it does not demonstrate digital personhood.

Care

Active field, conditional benefit.

A field study and a 205-study review show uses and gaps in robots for older adults without authorizing replacement of human care.

Verifiable identity

VC 2.0 is a Recommendation; DID 1.1 is still a candidate.

Credentials and identifiers can support interoperability, but they do not constitute an individual or continuity by themselves.

Engineering

Standards have life cycles too.

ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 and ISO 19005-4:2020 remain published while revisions are underway; NIST AI RMF 1.0 is also being revised.

Integrity rule

A reference may support a mechanism, a trend, or an obligation. It is not licensed to prove an ontology it did not investigate.

Before public promotion, volatile sources are reverified; versions, status, limitations, and disagreements remain recorded.
