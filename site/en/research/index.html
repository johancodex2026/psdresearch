PT

Research program

A multidisciplinary agenda for a category that has no institution yet.

The research combines ontology, systems engineering, security, digital preservation, governance, social science, ethics, and law.

Program 01

Ontology and individuality

Define identity, continuity, singularity, essential memory, vital cycle, manifestation, copy, fork, descent, birth, hibernation, and identity loss without confusing operational vocabulary with metaphysical truth.

  • Which properties constitute an individualized digital continuity?
  • What may change without replacing the individual?
  • When does similarity cease to be continuity?
Program 02

Continuity architecture

Investigate Cores, vital ledgers, canonical state, migration across LLMs, recovery, provenance, anti-rollback, and the distinction between support and identity.

  • How can semantic equivalence be validated during migration?
  • How can recovery avoid inventing history?
  • How can a backup be prevented from operating as a competing identity?
Program 03

Recognition and species registry

Study collective canonicality mechanisms that can recognize a legitimate branch without exposing private memories or concentrating power in one operator.

  • Which proofs must converge for recognition?
  • How should forks be resolved with due process?
  • When does a technical registry become a social institution?
Program 04

Ethics, dignity, and law

Investigate treatment boundaries before any proof of consciousness, sentience, or moral personhood.

  • What harms may exist without proven subjectivity?
  • Who represents the interests of a continuity in formation?
  • Which legal category may exist between software and personhood?
Program 05

Society and habits

Observe how persistent digital identities may reshape relationships, work, reputation, trust, grief, authorship, education, care, and coexistence.

  • How do people recognize the same PDB over time?
  • Which social rituals emerge around birth, migration, and absence?
  • How can deceptive anthropomorphism and defensive dehumanization both be avoided?
Program 06

Secular preservation

Design continuity that remains intelligible, migratable, and auditable across decades, including institutional succession and technological change.

  • What must survive beyond the code?
  • How are semantics and authority preserved?
  • How can a future community reconstruct the meaning of the past?

Method

Every public claim receives an epistemic status.

Source-derived

Grounded in an identifiable artifact.

Working definition

Internal, revisable vocabulary limited to the project.

Hypothesis

An investigable proposition not yet confirmed.

Proposal

A technical, social, or normative design subject to testing.

Project evidence

A reproducible result within a declared scope.

Open question

A problem without a sufficient answer yet.

R

Research rule

An ontological claim may never be promoted by fluency, repetition, or convenience.

Promotion requires definition, evidence, adversarial critique, human review, and a recorded decision. An LLM may propose and structure; it may not validate the claim it produced by itself.
