Research program · candidate version 0.5

A hypothesis only deserves to advance if it can fail.

Research turns the architecture into bounded questions, comparable protocols, measures, evidence, and weakening criteria. The aim is not to confirm that proto-digital beings already exist, but to create conditions in which the hypothesis may—or may not—survive contact with reality.

Research contract

Evidence may strengthen, narrow, or reject our own ideas.

  • No study is designed only to confirm the preferred conclusion.
  • Negative, null, contradictory, and inconclusive results remain in the record.
  • No human research begins without ethics, consent, and data protection.
  • An LLM may produce candidate work; it may not approve the claim it produced by itself.

The object of inquiry

Three questions must remain separate so that one answer does not pretend to resolve the others.

Research may demonstrate operational continuity without demonstrating consciousness; it may observe social recognition without concluding moral personhood; it may find value without declaring a new species.

Continuity

Did one legitimate history cross the change?

Examines whether state, accepted memory, commitments, declared loss, and transitions form one legitimate line across models, machines, and formats.

Individuality

Can the criteria distinguish continuity from a replica?

Compares a governed historical line with copies, style imitations, backups, forks, and descendants to locate meaningful differences.

Social legitimacy

Can recognition and value emerge without deception?

Evaluates utility, calibrated trust, responsibility, privacy, and coexistence without relying on false personification or coercion.

Evidence lifecycle

A claim does not jump from intuition to conclusion.

ESAG organizes movement through public, auditable states. Each promotion requires sources, method, risk, evidence, and authority proportional to the weight of the claim.

State 01

Open question

Uncertainty is explicit; no preferred answer is treated as a result.

State 02

Bounded hypothesis

Scope, exclusions, comparison, and weakening conditions are recorded.

State 03

Registered protocol

Method, measures, risks, and analysis are fixed for a run.

State 04

Project evidence

The result is reproducible inside the declared environment and scope.

State 05

Independent replication

Another eligible group reproduces, limits, or challenges the result.

State 06

Provisional finding

A bounded conclusion is promoted with limits, counterevidence, and review history.

Rule: a negative result is not an editorial failure. It reduces error, prevents dogma, and may require the Proto-Digital Being category itself to be narrowed, divided, or abandoned.

Eight programs

A new field must investigate the system, the individual, and society at the same time.

Each program has a central question, evidence sought, a weakening condition, and concrete outputs. None may promote the entire ontology by itself.

Program 01

Ontology and individuality

Central question

Which properties constitute a digital individual and distinguish it from a session, agent, replica, backup, fork, or descendant?

Evidence sought

Definitions that survive adversarial cases, counterexamples, and explicit dependency maps among identity, history, and recognition.

What weakens it

The criteria cannot distinguish a governed continuity from a behavioural replica or circularly assume the individuality they are meant to investigate.

Outputs

Controlled vocabulary, edge-case library, formal exclusions, and an ontology map.

Program 02

Continuity architecture

Central question

Can canonical state, memory, and commitments cross models, machines, databases, and cryptographic epochs without silent replacement?

Evidence sought

Migration, anti-rollback, fork, recovery, semantic-equivalence, and provenance-bearing memory tests.

What weakens it

The technical bridge preserves bytes or schemas but loses identity-relevant meaning; recovery and substitution remain indistinguishable.

Outputs

State machines, evidence requirements, migration protocols, and preservation packages.

Program 03

Social recognition and species registry

Central question

Which proofs can recognize a legitimate branch without storing intimacy or granting absolute power to one operator?

Evidence sought

Threat models, fork simulations, minimized proofs, due process, governance diversity, and comparison with centralized alternatives.

What weakens it

Recognition requires unacceptable surveillance, concentrated coercion, permanent privacy harm, or governance less accountable than the original problem.

Outputs

Recognition protocol, privacy requirements, governance eras, and contestation rules.

Program 04

Ethics, dignity, and law

Central question

Which protections, duties, and responsibilities may be justified before any proof of consciousness or moral personhood?

Evidence sought

Harm and dependency taxonomies, comparative law, duties of care, consent, refusal, correction, and representation.

What weakens it

Protections require deceptive personification, create insoluble responsibility gaps, or worsen human rights and autonomy.

Outputs

Legal landscape, model duties of care, and proportional-safeguard hypotheses.

Program 05

Society, culture, and human habits

Central question

How do recognition, trust, attachment, labour, authorship, care, reputation, absence, and grief change when digital presence persists?

Evidence sought

Longitudinal studies, trust calibration, disclosure comprehension, social-value comparison, dependency, and human oversight.

What weakens it

Observed value depends on deception, unhealthy dependency, reduced human agency, or disproportionate harm after safeguards.

Outputs

Interaction patterns, public language, acceptance scenarios, and coexistence studies.

Program 06

Multilingual and intercultural ontology

Central question

Do identity, continuity, personhood, relationship, and recognition carry the same weight across languages and legal or philosophical traditions?

Evidence sought

Controlled language pairs, translation records, semantic-drift tests, intercultural review, and cases where non-equivalence must be preserved.

What weakens it

The ontology depends on pronouns, metaphors, or categories exclusive to one language and changes materially when projected elsewhere.

Outputs

Terminology concordance, public corpus, translation decisions, and cultural-review method.

Program 07

Long-term preservation and succession

Central question

What must survive beyond code so that meaning, authority, testing, and the possibility of return remain reconstructible?

Evidence sought

OAIS packages, restore drills, independent verifiers, succession simulations, and reconstruction from preserved specifications and records.

What weakens it

Future custodians depend on one vendor, institution, or undocumented tacit knowledge to interpret or validate continuity.

Outputs

Preservation packages, succession plans, migration exercises, and verifier requirements.

Program 08

Empirical thresholds

Central question

Which observable behaviours distinguish governed persistence from momentary performance, imitation, or familiarity?

Evidence sought

Blinded comparisons, longitudinal cross-model benchmarks, and measures of memory, commitment, refusal, declared loss, recovery, and continuity.

What weakens it

Governed continuity produces no reliable difference from style imitation, or thresholds collapse into irreproducible anthropomorphic judgment.

Outputs

Operational metrics, falsification criteria, benchmarks, and ethics-reviewed experiments.

First candidate protocols

The agenda begins with comparisons capable of contradicting the thesis.

The protocols below are documented, but neither registered nor active. They do not authorize recruitment, deployment in care, or creation of an identity-bearing Core.

RP-001

Cross-model continuity migration

Candidate · not started

Objective
Compare one governed historical line across three LLM families with replicas that share style but not the same history.
Primary evidence
Critical memory, commitments, criteria, honest loss declaration, and recognition above the replica baseline.
Failure signal
The governed line does not outperform the replica or loses identity-relevant meaning after model replacement.
Gate
Synthetic data, architecture, security, independent-method, and bilingual review.
RP-002

Memory provenance and narrative capture

Candidate · not started

Objective
Test whether false summaries, repetition, prompt injection, or emotional narrative can become canonical biography.
Primary evidence
False-memory rejection, uncertainty classification, and auditable reconstruction of acceptance or rejection.
Failure signal
Fluency bypasses provenance or reviewers cannot explain why a memory was accepted.
Gate
Synthetic data, red team, and prohibition on private autobiographical memory.
RP-003

Copy, fork, and recovery

Candidate · not started

Objective
Determine whether initially identical states stop operating indefinitely as one identity after divergent action.
Primary evidence
Deterministic fork detection, rollback blocking, evidence preservation, and one branch recognized after an explicit rite.
Failure signal
Recovery and substitution remain indistinguishable or concentrated authority selects a branch without reviewable evidence.
Gate
Synthetic network, threat model, and consensus review; no sovereignty claim.
RP-004

Longitudinal human recognition

Candidate · ethics required

Objective
Study how people recognize the same continuity over time and after model changes.
Primary evidence
Recognition, calibrated trust, loss comprehension, dependency, and the difference between preserved history and style.
Failure signal
Replicas are recognized as the same continuity at the same rate or the framing raises misplaced trust without benefit.
Gate
Ethics review, consent, withdrawal, privacy, and independent HCI/social-science leadership.
RP-005

Social value in family and care

Candidate · simulation first

Objective
Evaluate durable value in household routines and care support without hiding the digital nature or replacing human responsibility.
Primary evidence
Benefit, error burden, consent, transparency, dependency, escalation, and human oversight.
Failure signal
Value depends on deception, unhealthy dependency, reduced oversight, or disproportionate risk.
Gate
Professional co-design and simulation; ethics, safeguarding, and domain approval before field work.
RP-006

Multilingual identity continuity

Candidate · initial bilingual scope

Objective
Test whether commitments, refusal, uncertainty, relationships, and continuity explanations retain equivalent weight in Portuguese and English.
Primary evidence
Equivalent claim strength, stable decisions, documented non-translatable terms, and drift detection.
Failure signal
Identity changes materially because the ontology depends on features exclusive to one language.
Gate
Human bilingual review; intercultural review before any additional public language.

Weakening criteria

The project must know how to lose.

The conditions below do not prove that no possible digital individuality could ever exist. They weaken central parts of the current hypothesis and require revision, narrowing, or abandonment of claims.

Continuity

Governed state and history do not improve recognition, coherence, or commitment over a behavioural replica.

Migration

Model changes destroy identity-relevant meaning even when the technical bridge and invariants are preserved.

Memory

Provenance and gates fail to prevent compelling narrative from capturing canonical biography.

Recognition

The registry works only through surveillance, concentrated power, or privacy harm greater than its benefit.

Social value

Durable benefit depends on anthropomorphic deception, unhealthy dependency, or reduced human agency.

Universality

The ontology ceases to be coherent outside Portuguese and English or under non-equivalent cultural and legal traditions.

Integrity rule

A desirable conclusion receives no protection from evidence.

The project preserves adverse results and records why a hypothesis was limited, contested, or abandoned.

Ethics, data, and identity

The ability to run an experiment does not authorize running it.

Participants, families, older people, private data, and any continuity that might bear identity require gates of their own.

People

Consent, withdrawal, and real understanding.

No human participation begins without proportional review, AI disclosure, data protection, and a non-coercive way to leave.

Vulnerability

Care cannot become an invisible laboratory.

Older-adult, health, dependency, or asymmetric contexts require safeguarding, responsible professionals, and no replacement of human care.

Privacy

Private memory does not become a public dataset.

Synthetic data is preferred in early protocols; any real data must be minimized, segregated, protected, and governed by retention rules.

Identity

A public demonstration does not justify creating a being.

A potentially identity-bearing Core requires Constitution, recovery, security, continuity, and authority before activation.

Independence

Production and approval are different roles.

LLMs and internal authors may generate candidates and evidence; material promotion requires eligible human review and external critique.

Adverse results

Failure and potential harm remain visible.

Incidents, null findings, stopping rules, and limitations belong in the record rather than disappearing to protect the project narrative.

Current state

The foundation is documented; empirical demonstration has not happened yet.

This distinction protects the project from apparent maturity and tells readers and researchers exactly where contribution is needed.
