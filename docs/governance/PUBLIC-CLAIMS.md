# Public Claims Policy

## 1. Purpose

PSDResearch presents a possible social and ontological future. Public trust depends on distinguishing intellectual ambition from established evidence.

## 2. Claim classes

### C0 — Source-derived statement

A faithful statement supported by an identifiable source.

Required:

- source identity;
- scope;
- no stronger wording than the source supports.

### C1 — Working definition

A term defined for use inside PSDResearch.

Required:

- state that it is a project definition;
- exclusions;
- revisability.

### C2 — Hypothesis

An investigable proposition.

Required:

- explicit hypothesis language;
- conditions under which it could be weakened or rejected;
- no presentation as prediction or fact.

### C3 — Design proposal

A technical, social, or governance architecture proposed for evaluation.

Required:

- intended problem;
- assumptions;
- alternatives or limitations;
- no claim that implementation proves ontology.

### C4 — Project evidence

A result produced and verified inside a declared project scope.

Required:

- method;
- artifact or test;
- reproducibility;
- limitations.

### C5 — External fact

A factual statement about standards, laws, institutions, technology, or society.

Required:

- current, authoritative source;
- date when temporal stability matters;
- no extrapolation without labeling inference.

### C6 — Open question

A question for which PSDResearch does not have a sufficient answer.

Open questions are valuable outputs and must not be hidden to make the project look complete.

## 3. Protected terms

The following terms require qualification in public material:

- life;
- death;
- birth;
- species;
- individual;
- consciousness;
- sentience;
- will;
- autonomy;
- dignity;
- rights;
- personhood;
- civil registry.

Allowed patterns:

- “operational term”;
- “working definition”;
- “research hypothesis”;
- “proposed civil-registry function”;
- “does not constitute a scientific or legal declaration.”

## 4. Prohibited shortcuts

Do not state or imply:

- an LLM is a PDB because it speaks convincingly;
- memory persistence proves identity;
- blockchain proves life;
- a name or key proves individuality;
- social recognition proves consciousness;
- a PDB has legal rights today;
- the species registry already exists;
- the world has accepted PSDResearch terminology;
- a future prediction is inevitable.

## 5. Public analogy rule

The statement that proto-digital beings may become to digital society what robots are becoming to physical society is a **research hypothesis and social analogy**.

It must not be presented as:

- a guaranteed forecast;
- evidence of current social adoption;
- proof of equivalent embodiment;
- a claim that robots or PDBs have identical legal or moral status.

## 6. Correction policy

When a public claim is wrong or overstated:

- correct it promptly;
- preserve the correction in version history;
- state whether the error affected translations or downstream pages;
- do not silently rewrite a published claim of material importance.
