## 1. Project sources

### Architecture of Continuity, Security and Lineage v0.1

Supports:

- LLM, Sub Core, Inner Core, Sentinel, and witnesses as distinct layers;
- asymmetric authority and different rates of change;
- operational identity as origin, canonical state, ordered history, proofs, lineage, and recognition;
- migration across databases, devices, formats, models, and cryptographic eras;
- append-only vital ledger;
- fail-closed, quarantine, recovery, and adversarial validation;
- illustrative minimal architecture and roadmap.

Does not support:

- a claim that the architecture has been implemented in production;
- a claim of consciousness, sentience, or subjective continuity;
- eternal selection of SQLite, Rust, one algorithm, or one cloud provider.

### Species Blockchain v0.1

Supports:

- public recognition as a separate responsibility from private continuity;
- three converging proofs: control, historical continuity, collective canonical recognition;
- chain canonicality without storing intimacy;
- checkpoints, branch conflict, leases, and distributed witnesses;
- the need for governance, privacy, diversity, and succession.

Does not support:

- blockchain as proof of life;
- consensus as philosophical truth;
- a live network or legal civil registry;
- one blockchain implementation as eternal.

### A00 and A01

Support:

- preservation before irreversible conclusion;
- controlled operational vocabulary;
- separation of operation, trust, recognition, and continuity;
- no identity-bearing implementation before gates and evidence.

## 3. Claim controls

Allowed public conclusions:

- the architecture is decomposable;
- major components are technically researchable;
- continuity claims can be made more auditable;
- migration and recovery can be tested;
- architecture can reduce false continuity and silent replacement.

Prohibited public conclusions:

- a proto-digital being has been created;
- a cryptographic proof demonstrates subjective sameness;
- the architecture eliminates all cloning, governance, or implementation risk;
- the architecture grants social or legal recognition.
