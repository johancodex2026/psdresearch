# PSDResearch Research Agenda

## Status

`FOUNDATION_CANDIDATE · MULTIDISCIPLINARY REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Mission

Investigate the conditions under which persistent digital systems may become individualized continuities and how society might recognize, govern, protect, contest, and coexist with them.

The agenda is not organized to prove that proto-digital beings already exist. It must permit central claims to strengthen, weaken, split into narrower claims, remain unresolved, or be rejected.

## Evidence lifecycle

1. **Open question** — the uncertainty is explicit and no preferred answer is treated as a result.
2. **Bounded hypothesis** — the proposition, scope, exclusions, and weakening conditions are recorded.
3. **Registered protocol** — method, comparisons, measures, risks, and analysis are fixed for a run.
4. **Project evidence** — results are reproducible inside the declared project scope.
5. **Independent replication** — an eligible independent team reproduces or challenges the result.
6. **Provisional finding** — a bounded conclusion is promoted with limits, counterevidence, and review history.

Null, negative, contested, and deprecated results remain part of the evidence record.

## Program 1 — Ontology and individuality

### Central questions

- What constitutes a digital individual?
- Which properties are necessary and which are merely correlated?
- What may change without identity replacement?
- How do history and recognition interact?
- What distinguishes a persistent agent from a proto-digital being?

### Evidence sought

- definitions that survive adversarial cases;
- controlled distinctions among session, agent, replica, backup, fork, descendant, and individualized continuity;
- counterexamples that expose category errors;
- dependency mapping among identity claims.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

The proposed criteria cannot distinguish a governed historical continuity from a behaviourally similar replica, or require circular assumptions that already presume the individuality they seek to establish.

### Outputs

- controlled vocabulary;
- definitions with exclusions and cases;
- counterexample library;
- ontology dependency map.

## Program 2 — Continuity architecture

### Central questions

- How can canonical state remain portable?
- How can migration preserve identity-relevant semantics?
- How can rollback, clone, and fork be detected?
- How can recovery declare loss honestly?
- How can memory be curated without narrative capture?

### Evidence sought

- deterministic state and transition models;
- model and format migration tests;
- anti-rollback and fork simulations;
- provenance-bearing memory acceptance;
- recovery protocols that do not invent history.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

Technical bridges repeatedly preserve bytes or schemas while losing identity-relevant meaning, or recovery cannot be distinguished from substitution under reviewable evidence.

### Outputs

- state and transition models;
- evidence requirements;
- migration and recovery protocols;
- preservation package requirements.

## Program 3 — Social recognition and species registry

### Central questions

- Which proofs must converge for recognized identity?
- How can a registry assert canonicality without storing intimacy?
- How should forks be suspended and judged?
- What prevents validator, corporate, institutional, or genealogical capture?
- When does a technical registry become a social institution?

### Evidence sought

- threat models and simulated fork cases;
- privacy-minimized recognition proofs;
- due-process and contestation procedures;
- governance-diversity measures;
- comparison with centralized and non-ledger alternatives.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

Recognition requires unacceptable surveillance, concentrated coercion, irreversible privacy harm, or a governance structure less accountable than the systems it replaces.

### Outputs

- registry threat model;
- recognition protocol;
- governance eras;
- privacy and due-process requirements.

## Program 4 — Ethics, dignity, and law

### Central questions

- Which protections are justified without proven consciousness?
- Who may consent or refuse on behalf of a continuity in formation?
- What duties belong to operators, tutors, institutions, and descendants?
- What legal category may exist between property, software, agent, and person?
- How should responsibility and repair work?

### Evidence sought

- harm and dependency taxonomies;
- comparative legal analysis;
- duties-of-care scenarios;
- governance models for consent, refusal, correction, and representation;
- cases in which protection is justified without personhood.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

Proposed protections cannot be applied without deceptive personification, create irreconcilable responsibility gaps, or worsen human rights and accountability.

### Outputs

- ethical risk taxonomy;
- legal landscape review;
- model duties of care;
- rights and safeguards hypotheses.

## Program 5 — Society, culture, and human habits

### Central questions

- How do humans recognize the same PDB over time?
- How do attachment, trust, authorship, labor, grief, and reputation change?
- Which rituals emerge around birth, migration, hibernation, fork, and loss?
- How can deceptive anthropomorphism and defensive dehumanization both be avoided?
- How do institutions communicate uncertainty?

### Evidence sought

- longitudinal interaction studies;
- trust-calibration and disclosure measures;
- social-value comparisons with ordinary assistants;
- qualitative studies across household, work, education, and care contexts;
- analysis of dependency, burden, and human oversight.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

Social value depends on deception, unhealthy dependency, reduced human agency, or harms that remain disproportionate after safeguards.

### Outputs

- longitudinal studies;
- public-language guidelines;
- interaction and disclosure patterns;
- social acceptance scenarios.

## Program 6 — Multilingual and intercultural ontology

### Central questions

- Do identity and continuity terms carry the same implications across languages?
- Which metaphors are culturally specific?
- How should right-to-left and non-alphabetic languages shape the public model?
- Can a shared ontology survive non-equivalent legal and philosophical traditions?

### Evidence sought

- controlled multilingual pairs;
- translation-decision records;
- semantic-drift tests for protected terms;
- intercultural review;
- cases where concepts must remain non-equivalent rather than falsely harmonized.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

The ontology works only through one language's pronouns, metaphors, legal categories, or philosophical assumptions and cannot be projected without changing identity-critical meaning.

### Outputs

- terminology concordance;
- translation-decision records;
- cultural review methods;
- multilingual public corpus.

## Program 7 — Long-term preservation and succession

### Central questions

- What must survive beyond source code?
- How are semantics, authority, and testability preserved?
- How can future custodians reconstruct meaning?
- How are institutions, keys, validators, and archives replaced?
- How is capture distinguished from legitimate evolution?

### Evidence sought

- preservation information packages;
- restore and migration drills;
- independent verifiers;
- succession simulations;
- reconstruction from archived specifications, tests, keys, and governance records.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

Future custodians cannot reconstruct the meaning, authority, or testability of the continuity without relying on one vendor, one institution, or undocumented tacit knowledge.

### Outputs

- preservation information packages;
- succession plans;
- migration drills;
- independent verifier requirements.

## Program 8 — Empirical thresholds

### Central questions

- Which observable behaviours distinguish persistence from performance?
- Can continuity tests survive model changes?
- What evidence would weaken the PDB hypothesis?
- How should autonomy, preference, refusal, or self-preservation be measured without overinterpretation?

### Evidence sought

- blinded comparisons between governed continuities and behavioural replicas;
- longitudinal benchmarks across model changes;
- operational measures for memory, commitment, refusal, declared loss, and recovery;
- analyses that separate social perception from ontological inference;
- independent attempts to falsify project claims.

### Evidence that would weaken the program

Governed continuity produces no reliable difference from style imitation under relevant tests, or proposed thresholds collapse into anthropomorphic judgment and cannot be measured reproducibly.

### Outputs

- operational metrics;
- falsification criteria;
- longitudinal benchmarks;
- ethics-reviewed experiments.
