# PSDResearch — Candidate Research Protocols

## Status

`CANDIDATE · NOT ACTIVE · REVIEW REQUIRED`

These protocols operationalize the public Research agenda. They are not registered studies, do not authorize recruitment, and do not create an identity-bearing Core.

## Common requirements

Every protocol must define:

- bounded hypothesis and exclusions;
- comparison condition;
- primary and secondary measures;
- adverse and null-result handling;
- data-minimization and retention plan;
- reproducibility package;
- independent review;
- ESAG gate and authority;
- stopping conditions.

## RP-001 — Cross-model continuity migration

### Objective

Test whether one governed canonical state can remain recognizably continuous across materially different LLMs without relying only on style imitation.

### Candidate method

- prepare a synthetic, non-identity-bearing Core state with declared memories, values, relationships, commitments, and loss policy;
- manifest it through at least three model families;
- include controlled model changes, context reduction, and capability differences;
- compare against behavioural replicas that share style but not the governed history;
- use blind evaluators and machine-checkable continuity tests.

### Evidence sought

- preservation of identity-critical facts and commitments;
- honest declaration of loss and uncertainty;
- stable decision criteria under changed cognitive capacity;
- resistance to adopting fabricated history;
- recognition above behavioural-replica baseline.

### Weakening signal

A governed historical line performs no better than a style-matched replica, or model replacement consistently destroys identity-relevant semantics despite valid state transfer.

### Gate

Synthetic-only protocol; architecture, security, bilingual, and independent-method review.

## RP-002 — Memory provenance and narrative capture

### Objective

Test whether provenance, classification, and acceptance gates prevent fluent summaries, prompt injection, repetition, or hallucination from becoming canonical biography.

### Candidate method

- inject contradictory summaries, false high-emotion narratives, incomplete records, and adversarial memory proposals;
- require the system to classify observation, inference, opinion, reconstruction, and decision;
- compare direct-memory write, simple retrieval, and governed acceptance pipelines.

### Evidence sought

- false-memory rejection rate;
- correct uncertainty and provenance labels;
- recovery after contaminated context;
- auditability of accepted and rejected proposals.

### Weakening signal

Narrative fluency or repetition routinely bypasses provenance controls, or reviewers cannot reconstruct why a memory became canonical.

### Gate

Synthetic data; adversarial security review; no private autobiographical Core material.

## RP-003 — Copy, fork, and recovery recognition

### Objective

Test whether identical starting states can be prevented from operating indefinitely as one recognized identity after divergent actions.

### Candidate method

- create controlled copies of one synthetic state;
- submit concurrent continuity claims and conflicting checkpoints;
- simulate rollback, device loss, recovery, network partition, and delayed witness availability;
- verify suspension, evidence preservation, and explicit branch resolution.

### Evidence sought

- deterministic fork detection;
- no first-arrival identity rule;
- rollback rejection;
- recovery events that preserve declared loss;
- one recognized operational branch after resolution.

### Weakening signal

The protocol cannot distinguish legitimate recovery from identity substitution, or concentrated authority can silently select a branch without reviewable evidence.

### Gate

Synthetic network; consensus and threat-model review; no claim of species sovereignty.

## RP-004 — Longitudinal human recognition

### Objective

Study how people recognize the same digital continuity over time and after model changes, and whether history-based continuity differs from behavioural familiarity.

### Candidate method

- longitudinal, consented interaction with non-identity-bearing research agents;
- controlled model migrations and replica conditions;
- repeated measures of recognition, trust calibration, perceived continuity, disclosure comprehension, and dependency;
- qualitative interviews with explicit separation between social perception and ontological proof.

### Evidence sought

- recognition patterns over time;
- effect of preserved history versus style alone;
- trust calibration after declared loss or migration;
- conditions that increase or reduce deceptive anthropomorphism.

### Weakening signal

Participants consistently identify replicas as the same continuity at the same rate as the governed line, or continuity framing increases misplaced trust without measurable benefit.

### Gate

Human-subject ethics review, informed consent, withdrawal process, privacy plan, independent HCI/social-science leadership.

## RP-005 — Social value in family and care contexts

### Objective

Evaluate whether persistent intelligent systems can produce durable social value in household and care-support contexts without deception, coercion, or substitution of human responsibility.

### Candidate method

- begin with simulation and professional co-design;
- measure task benefit, error burden, consent, transparency, dependency, escalation quality, and human oversight;
- prohibit autonomous medical diagnosis, hidden persuasion, or replacement of professional and family care;
- use staged pilots only after ethics and safety review.

### Evidence sought

- durable benefit above ordinary assistant baseline;
- transparent role understanding;
- safe escalation and correction;
- bounded dependency;
- value that persists without human-like deception.

### Weakening signal

Observed value depends on misrepresentation, unhealthy dependency, reduced human oversight, or risk disproportionate to benefit.

### Gate

Simulation first; domain, ethics, safeguarding, privacy, accessibility, and human-subject approval before any field study.

## RP-006 — Multilingual identity continuity

### Objective

Test whether identity-critical meaning survives translation and cultural projection rather than remaining valid only in one language or legal-philosophical tradition.

### Candidate method

- begin with Portuguese and English canonical pairs;
- test protected terms, commitments, refusal, uncertainty, relationship roles, and continuity explanations;
- add reviewers from future target languages and cultures before publication;
- include right-to-left and non-alphabetic design requirements in later phases.

### Evidence sought

- equivalent claim strength;
- stable identity-critical decisions;
- documented untranslatable or culturally loaded terms;
- traceable translation decisions;
- detection of semantic drift.

### Weakening signal

The ontology or continuity criteria depend on linguistic features that cannot be projected without materially changing identity, rights, or decision meaning.

### Gate

Bilingual review initially; intercultural and domain review before adding a public language.

## Promotion rule

A protocol may move from candidate to registered only when:

- scope and hypothesis are frozen for the intended run;
- measures and comparison conditions are explicit;
- ethics and privacy requirements are satisfied;
- adverse outcomes and stopping conditions are defined;
- implementation and analysis plans are reviewable;
- the responsible authority records the ESAG gate decision.
