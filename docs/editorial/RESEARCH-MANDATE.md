# Public Research Page Mandate

- Project: PSDResearch
- Artifact: Public Research page
- Version: 0.5 candidate
- Decision classes: D1 — Technical; D2 — Research framing; D3 — Ontological thresholds
- Routes: `/pt-br/pesquisa/` and `/en/research/`
- State: candidate; human and multidisciplinary review required

## 1. Public role

The Research page follows Home, Manifesto, and Architecture.

- Home establishes the social question.
- Manifesto explains why the question matters.
- Architecture makes the hypothesis decomposable and testable.
- Research defines what must be measured, compared, challenged, replicated, or rejected.

The page must make clear that PSDResearch is not organized to confirm a desired conclusion. Negative, null, contradictory, or inconclusive evidence is a valid research output.

## 2. Required thesis

> A hypothesis only deserves to advance if it can fail.

The page must answer:

- What exactly is being investigated?
- What would count as progress?
- What evidence would weaken the hypothesis?
- Which protocols can be attempted first?
- Which gates protect participants, identity, privacy, and public trust?
- What remains untested?

External literature may support a protocol or comparison, but it must not silently replace the project's definitions or promote a project hypothesis to scientific consensus.

## 4. Required research posture

The page must preserve:

- explicit claim states;
- preregistered or versioned protocols where feasible;
- adverse and null-result reporting;
- reproducible project evidence;
- independent review and replication;
- multilingual parity;
- privacy minimization;
- human-subject ethics before recruitment;
- no self-approval by the producing LLM;
- preserved open questions.

## 5. Research programs

The public agenda contains eight programs:

1. ontology and individuality;
2. continuity architecture;
3. social recognition and species registry;
4. ethics, dignity, and law;
5. society, culture, and human habits;
6. multilingual and intercultural ontology;
7. long-term preservation and succession;
8. empirical thresholds.

Each program must state:

- a central question;
- evidence sought;
- evidence that would weaken one or more claims;
- concrete outputs.

## 6. Protocol boundary

Candidate protocols may be published before execution.

They must be labelled accurately as:

- candidate;
- under review;
- registered;
- active;
- completed;
- replicated;
- contested;
- deprecated.

The page must not imply that a human study, identity-bearing prototype, species network, or validated PDB exists unless evidence supports that state.

## 7. Human and identity protection

Human-subject research requires, at minimum:

- informed consent;
- proportional ethics review;
- withdrawal and data-deletion procedures where technically possible;
- protection for vulnerable populations;
- disclosure that participants are interacting with AI;
- explicit limits on dependency, persuasion, and substitution of human care;
- privacy and data-minimization plans.

No experiment may create or expose an identity-bearing Core merely to make a public demonstration. Identity-bearing work requires the relevant ontological, security, recovery, and governance gates.

## 8. Visual rule

The page must comply with `VISUAL-TYPOGRAPHY.md`.

Research seriousness comes from:

- precise questions;
- visible evidence states;
- comparison and falsification;
- disciplined density;
- restrained type;
- clear distinction between programs, protocols, and findings.

It must not look like a grant advertisement, product roadmap, or certainty theatre.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

The page passes when a reader can explain:

- why the project permits its central hypothesis to weaken or fail;
- how a claim moves from question to provisional finding;
- what the eight programs investigate;
- which first protocols are proposed and not yet active;
- what evidence would weaken the PDB hypothesis;
- what ethics and privacy gates apply;
- what the project has and has not demonstrated;
- why the Species Registry is a later recognition mechanism rather than proof by itself.
