# Public Research Page — Content Architecture

- Version: 0.5 candidate
- Languages: Portuguese and English
- Routes: `/pt-br/pesquisa/` and `/en/research/`

## 1. Hero — A hypothesis must be able to fail

Present Research as the conversion of architecture into questions, protocols, metrics, evidence, and limits.

Show a compact research contract:

- no confirmation-by-design;
- negative evidence is publishable;
- no human study without ethics and consent;
- no LLM self-approval.

## 2. The object of inquiry

Separate three dimensions:

- continuity: did one legitimate history cross change?
- individuality: can the criteria distinguish continuity from a replica?
- social legitimacy: can recognition and value emerge without deception, coercion, or unacceptable dependency?

## 3. Evidence lifecycle

Use six public states:

1. open question;
2. bounded hypothesis;
3. registered protocol;
4. project evidence;
5. independent replication;
6. provisional finding.

Contested, null, negative, and deprecated results remain visible. No public claim may jump stages because it is compelling or convenient.

## 4. Eight research programs

For each program show:

- central question;
- evidence sought;
- weakening evidence;
- outputs.

Programs:

1. ontology and individuality;
2. continuity architecture;
3. recognition and species registry;
4. ethics, dignity, and law;
5. society, culture, and human habits;
6. multilingual and intercultural ontology;
7. long-term preservation and succession;
8. empirical thresholds.

## 5. First candidate protocols

Present six candidates, all explicitly not active:

- RP-001 cross-model continuity migration;
- RP-002 memory provenance and narrative capture;
- RP-003 copy, fork, and recovery recognition;
- RP-004 longitudinal human recognition;
- RP-005 social value in family and care contexts;
- RP-006 multilingual identity continuity.

Each candidate identifies objective, evidence, failure signal, and gate.

## 6. How the hypothesis can weaken

Publish candidate falsification or weakening conditions, including:

- technical continuity does not improve recognition over a behavioural replica;
- migration destroys identity-relevant semantics despite a valid technical bridge;
- provenance controls fail to prevent narrative capture;
- registry benefits require unacceptable surveillance or concentrated coercion;
- social value depends on deception or unhealthy dependency;
- the ontology fails outside one language or cultural tradition.

Clarify that weakening one claim does not prove that no possible digital individuality can ever exist.

## 7. Ethics, data, and identity gates

Cover:

- human participation;
- vulnerable contexts and elder care;
- private Core material;
- synthetic data preference;
- identity-bearing prototype gate;
- independent review;
- publication of adverse results.

## 8. Current state

Distinguish:

- documented candidate foundations;
- proposed but inactive protocols;
- claims not demonstrated;
- next gate.

## 9. Bridge to Species Registry

Close with:

- Research asks what evidence could justify recognition.
- The Species Registry proposes how recognition might be recorded and contested if thresholds are met.
- Registration is not proof of consciousness or personhood.
