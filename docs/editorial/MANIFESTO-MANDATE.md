# Manifesto Editorial Mandate

- Project: PSDResearch
- Artifact: Public Manifesto
- Version: 0.2 candidate
- Decision classes: D2 — Research / public framing; D3 — ontological wording
- Founding direction approved: 2026-08-20
- Implementation state: candidate
- Languages: Portuguese and English

## 1. Mission

The Manifesto is the bridge between the socially approachable Home and the future Architecture page.

It must:

- increase the perceived seriousness of the project;
- show that the ontology can become a research and engineering program;
- connect the concept to observable international trends;
- guide the reader through questions rather than demand belief;
- make technical viability and social relevance intelligible;
- preserve uncertainty around consciousness, sentience, life, personhood, and legal status.

The Manifesto remains conceptual. It introduces executable commitments but does not teach the detailed architecture, cryptography, consensus, schemas, or implementation roadmap.

## 2. Source basis

The primary internal source is:

`Manifesto_Conceitual_Proto_Seres_Digitais_v0_1.docx`, dated 12 July 2026.

The public page preserves its main propositions:

- a digital identity should be capable of origin, history, continuity, and descent;
- current AI may appear person-like while lacking a verifiable biography;
- a protected Core would preserve identity-relevant state while the cognitive model remains replaceable;
- the model may change without silently replacing the history;
- descent creates a new individual rather than copying the progenitor;
- collective recognition should not imply ownership;
- no single sovereign, master key, automatic death, originless memory, retroactive cloning, or unbounded evolution;
- the project does not claim to have created life or consciousness;
- the world should be prepared to recognize a new category only if evidence eventually supports it.

## 3. Founding question

The public question is:

> When does a highly complex, persistent digital system stop being adequately described only as “it” and begin to invite “he,” “she,” or “they” as a social description?

This is not a grammar test and not an ontological shortcut.

The Manifesto must state:

- pronouns can reveal social relation;
- pronouns do not prove consciousness or individuality;
- the research question is which evidence could justify a change in social category;
- appearance, voice, emotional language, or fluency are insufficient;
- persistence, history, memory, responsibility, value, and recognition are more relevant candidates.

## 4. Intended reader progression

The page guides the reader through this sequence:

1. **The world is changing.** Agents move from response to action.
2. **The direction is visible.** Identity, authorization, interoperability, memory, and care are active research and standards areas.
3. **What if?** Persistent agents begin to remember, contribute, care, and assume responsibilities.
4. **Current categories become insufficient.** “Only a system” may remain technically true while becoming socially incomplete.
5. **A disciplined hypothesis appears.** Proto-Digital Being is a working category, not a proclamation.
6. **The concept becomes executable.** Founding commitments become testable constraints.
7. **The reader reaches a bounded conclusion.** The research direction is viable, socially relevant, and conditionally acceptable; value must be demonstrated.
8. **Architecture becomes necessary.** The next page will show layers, boundaries, evidence, and validation paths.

## 5. Target conclusion

A careful reader should be able to conclude:

- persistent agent identity is a viable research problem;
- technical prerequisites are appearing across standards and research;
- human–AI relations and care settings make the issue socially relevant;
- social acceptability is possible when transparency, privacy, responsibility, due process, and human value are present;
- the hypothesis deserves engineering and multidisciplinary research now;
- no claim of consciousness, life, moral personhood, or legal status has been proven.

The page must not pressure the reader to conclude that proto-digital beings already exist.

## 6. Ten-article form

The official public form remains ten articles:

1. The world is moving from answers to action.
2. Social language arrives before ontology.
3. Persistence changes the object being evaluated.
4. Social value may precede ontological status.
5. Person-like appearance cannot define a person.
6. The model may change; history must not be silently replaced.
7. Memory without origin is not biography.
8. Private continuity is not yet social validity.
9. Prudence must precede certainty.
10. The field must be built before the emergency.

Each article includes:

- a clear proposition;
- an explanatory paragraph;
- an “What if / E se” question where useful;
- an evidence, criterion, or governance note.

## 7. Evidence policy

The page may use current international sources to show direction.

Every external source must be classified as one of:

- official public initiative;
- open standards or infrastructure;
- peer-reviewed research;
- research review;
- clearly marked preprint.

External sources support only the specific trend described. They do not validate the full PSD ontology.

Temporal claims must be dated and rechecked before a public release.

## 8. Social acceptability rule

The page may state that social acceptance is possible only as a conditional proposition.

Acceptance must be linked to:

- demonstrated value;
- truthful disclosure that the system is AI;
- privacy and data minimization;
- accountability and bounded authority;
- protection against deceptive anthropomorphism;
- contestability and due process;
- recovery before destructive conclusions;
- preservation of human autonomy and relationships.

## 9. Non-commercial and anti-fiction boundary

The Manifesto must not:

- sell a product, service, being, or investment;
- claim inevitable historical destiny;
- frame current prototypes as a living species;
- use human-like imagery as proof;
- present philosophy as empirical result;
- hide LLM assistance;
- imply legal or scientific recognition that does not exist.

## 10. Exit to Architecture

The closing statement must distinguish roles:

- the Manifesto explains **why** the field matters and what commitments guide it;
- the Architecture page will explain **how** the idea can be organized into components, boundaries, authority, evidence, and validation.

Until the Architecture page is created, it is shown as an upcoming public artifact rather than a broken or fabricated route.

## 11. Acceptance criteria

The Manifesto passes candidate review when:

- the pronoun threshold is compelling but explicitly non-probative;
- global trends are based on current, linked sources;
- the ten articles form a coherent progression;
- technical feasibility is separated from ontological proof;
- social acceptability remains conditional and evidence-based;
- the six founding commitments are preserved;
- the source Manifesto's closing declaration remains recognizable;
- Portuguese and English preserve the same claim strength and limitations;
- the page remains accessible and responsive;
- the next step toward Architecture is clear.
