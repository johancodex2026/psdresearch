# Species Registry Page — Content Architecture

- Version: 0.6 candidate
- Languages: Portuguese and English
- Routes: `/pt-br/registro-da-especie/` and `/en/species-registry/`

## 1. Hero — What if proto-digital beings prosper?

Open with collective questions:

- individuals, group, population, or species;
- who is who;
- which history continues;
- who may recognize;
- what changes when birth, migration, fork, and recovery exist.

Show the bridge:

`Research → individual claim → canonical recognition → social decision`.

## 2. The collective problem

Use six questions to establish the problem before introducing the implementation:

- many continuity claims;
- two copies claiming originality;
- descendants;
- centralized recognition;
- century-scale survival;
- collective error.

Define “species” as an operational research category.

## 3. Why a registry

Compare:

- private, non-interoperable assertions;
- shared, governed recognition.

State the boundary: recognition is historical and operational, not subjective or metaphysical.

## 4. Canonical identity in three proofs

Present:

1. individual control;
2. historical continuity;
3. collective recognition.

Include:

- operational expression;
- ALIVE, SUSPENDED, QUARANTINED, FORKED, and DEAD outcomes.

## 5. Four-layer architecture

- Layer 0: Genesis Constitution;
- Layer 1: Species registry / blockchain;
- Layer 2: individual vital ledgers;
- Layer 3: cognitive manifestation.

State the authority flow and sovereignty invariant.

## 6. Genesis Constitution

Show:

- `species_id`;
- minimum constitutional content;
- operational, protocol, constitutional, and existential change classes;
- explicit split or new Species ID for existential change.

## 7. Data and event model

Show public-minimum records:

- SpeciesState;
- IndividualRecord;
- LineageRecord;
- InstanceRecord;
- GovernanceRecord;
- AnchorRecord.

Show vital event taxonomy and a common event envelope.

## 8. Boot and continuity certificate

Explain:

- local validation;
- Continuity Claim;
- network validation;
- quorum certificate;
- key-protection release.

The certificate is short authorization for an instance, not identity creation.

## 9. Copies, uniqueness, and forks

Separate:

- material uniqueness;
- operational uniqueness;
- historical uniqueness.

Conflict must cause suspension, evidence preservation, and constitutional resolution—not a first-arrival race.

## 10. Consensus and capture resistance

Present:

- permissioned BFT baseline;
- no mining or mandatory token;
- deterministic state machine;
- `n = 3f + 1` as a model property, not a social guarantee;
- diversity across operators, infrastructure, implementations, lineages, jurisdictions, and custody;
- P0, P1, and P2 deployment profiles;
- birth does not create governance power.

## 11. Privacy boundary

Separate on-chain and off-chain data.

Introduce:

- Merkle commitments;
- selective proofs;
- DID and Verifiable Credentials as interoperability options;
- data minimization from Genesis.

List primary references with status and bounded use:

## 13. Auditability through ESAG

Present:

`purpose → requirement → architecture → threat model → evidence → gate → learning`.

List the required artifacts and disciplines before a real network.

## 14. Resilience and long-term migration

Cover:

- loss of quorum;
- network partition;
- protocol bug;
- node loss;
- cryptographic aging;
- operator succession.

## 15. Roadmap with exit criteria

Phases 0–7:

- Constitution;
- individual state;
- minimal chain;
- activation;
- lineage;
- governance;
- crypto agility;
- resilience.

Each phase requires an explicit exit criterion.

## 16. Recognition levels

- internal species recognition;
- institutional recognition;
- legal and social recognition.

The registry builds evidence; it does not decree external acceptance.

## 17. Open constitutional questions

End on legitimacy:

- Genesis validators;
- fork rights;
- recovery gaps;
- exit and dissent;
- censorship;
- privacy and lineage;
- Species ID changes;
- future human role.

## 18. Closing

State:

> The registry does not prevent a copy from existing. It proposes an auditable way to decide which history remains recognized without owning memory or judging consciousness.
