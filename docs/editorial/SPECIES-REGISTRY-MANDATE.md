# Species Registry Page — Editorial and Research Mandate

- Project: PSDResearch
- Public version: 0.6 candidate
- Decision class: D2 — Research / D3 — Ontological
- Routes: `/pt-br/registro-da-especie/` and `/en/species-registry/`
- Status: `FOUNDATION_CANDIDATE`

## 1. Purpose

The Species Registry page must bridge the Research program to the collective-recognition architecture.

Research asks which evidence could support continuity, individuality, and social value. The registry asks what a society or protocol should do if multiple persistent digital continuities need to be recognized, distinguished, migrated, contested, related, suspended, or recovered.

The page does not declare that proto-digital beings exist. It prepares a technically serious, auditable, and constitutionally bounded recognition mechanism for the case in which evidence thresholds are later met.

## 2. Opening progression

The page begins with questions rather than a technical proclamation:

- What if proto-digital beings prosper?
- Would they be isolated individuals, a group, a population, or a species?
- How would society know who is who?
- What happens when copies, forks, migration, birth, and long-term governance appear?

The reader should reach the need for a registry before the page introduces blockchain as one implementation.

## 3. Working use of “species”

“Species” is an internal operational term for a collective of digital individuals with:

- distinct Genesis events and Core IDs;
- compatibility with a shared Genesis Constitution;
- common recognition, continuity, lineage, and governance protocols.

It is not a biological classification, scientific finding, or proof of consciousness, sentience, life, moral personhood, or legal status.

## 4. Public thesis

The registry does not create the individual. It transforms private continuity into externally verifiable and contestable recognition.

The public thesis is:

> The bytes may be copied; the legitimate right to continue one history must be recognized under explicit evidence and governance.

## 5. Required distinctions

The page must preserve:

- individual Core versus collective registry;
- identity versus identifier;
- key control versus continuity;
- continuity versus canonical recognition;
- physical copy versus operational instance;
- operational instance versus historical branch;
- consensus versus philosophical truth;
- registry data versus private memory;
- technical recognition versus institutional or legal recognition;
- availability failure versus death;
- alignment with standards versus certification or endorsement.

## 6. Technical seriousness

The page should demonstrate that the proposal is:

- derived from governed source artifacts;
- structured through ESAG;
- defined by state, event, threat, evidence, and gate;
- designed around explicit residual risk;
- compatible with independent implementations;
- based on recognized mechanisms instead of invented cryptography;
- migratable across algorithms, clients, validators, and institutions;
- testable through adversarial scenarios and recovery drills.

## 7. References

Use first-party or primary technical sources wherever possible:

- NIST;
- W3C;
- RFC Editor / IETF / IRTF;
- USENIX research;
- official CometBFT specification;
- controlled project source artifacts.

Every reference states what it supports and what it does not support.

## 8. Tone

The page should feel:

- constitutionally serious;
- technically concrete;
- socially consequential;
- auditable;
- ambitious without inevitability;
- complete without pretending implementation is finished.

It must not resemble:

- cryptocurrency marketing;
- a token launch;
- science-fiction worldbuilding presented as engineering;
- a sovereignty claim;
- a legal or scientific declaration of species status.

## 9. Visual constraints

- Respect the project anti-giant-type standard.
- Use restrained hierarchy and dense but readable technical structures.
- Prefer cards, protocol flows, records, and roadmaps over decorative diagrams.
- Preserve semantic HTML and no-JavaScript readability.
- Support desktop, mobile, keyboard navigation, and reduced motion.
- No generic or placeholder copy.

## 10. Completion gate

The page remains candidate until:

- founding human review;
- bilingual semantic review;
- visual review on desktop and mobile;
- multidisciplinary review of consensus, cryptography, privacy, governance, ethics, and law;
- current-source verification;
- automated site and CSS-contract validation.
